There's a Debt,
and There's a Way

A story about family, financial pressure, and why debt is a problem to solve — not a measure of a person’s worth.

Read aloud using your browser's built-in voice.

In a busy part of the city lived Samir's family. Samir ran a small business. His wife Mita, his son Arnab, and his daughter Tuli made up their household.

For a while, the business did quite well. Hoping for an even better future, Samir kept taking one loan after another to expand it.

At first, everything went smoothly. But then the business suffered a major loss.

On one side, the business's money got stuck. On the other, month after month, the loan repayments piled up. The interest kept growing. The phone calls started. Creditors began pressuring him to return the money.

Samir slowly began to break down. The man who once laughed and joked with his family now sat silently.

"What's wrong with you?"Mita "Everything is over. How will I ever repay so much money?"Samir "Nothing is over. The money is gone, but we are still here."Mita, calmly

Samir said nothing in reply.

The Pressure Kept Growing

In the weeks that followed, things grew harder still. Someone would call demanding payment; someone would come to the house asking for money. Samir grew afraid to even step outside.

"How will I show my face to people?"Samir, to himself

One day his son Arnab said to him, "Dad, why are you worrying so much?"

Samir didn't want to say anything at first. Then he said, "I've taken on a great deal of debt."

Arnab said, "Then we'll all figure something out together."

Samir looked at his son, startled. "What will you figure out?"

Arnab said, "I don't know. But why does anyone have to bear everything alone?"

Those words left a mark on Samir's heart.

A Mistaken Belief

Samir had believed, all this time, that debt meant the end of dignity. That debt meant failure. That debt meant there was no way forward.

But reality was different. Debt is a financial problem — it is not the end of a life.

The next day, Mita took him to see a lawyer and a financial advisor they knew. They wrote out every debt, in full — where money was owed, how much, at what interest, which repayment needed attention first, where more time could be requested. Everything was laid out and organized.

"Your problem is a big one. But a big problem doesn't mean an unsolvable one."the advisor

Samir listened quietly.

The Creditors' Pressure

Not every creditor spoke the same way, of course. Some agreed to give more time. Some agreed to work out a repayment plan. And some tried, without cause, to intimidate him.

This time, Samir made no decision alone. Wherever it was needed, he corresponded in writing. He gathered the necessary documents. He sought legal advice.

He came to understand — facing a problem laid out clearly on paper is far more effective than running from the pressure of it.

The Family's Strength

One night, the four of them sat together.

"We may not be able to live the way we used to."Mita "So what if we can't?"Arnab "Dad, we still have you."Tuli, smiling

Tears came to Samir's eyes. He realized that, all this time, he had only been looking at the arithmetic of his debt — and had forgotten to see the four lives, the four relationships, and the one future standing right in front of him.

A Few Years Later

Time moves forward, however slowly. Samir closed part of the business. He cut unnecessary expenses. The whole family sat down together and planned anew.

Some assets had to be sold. Life became far simpler. But the family stayed together.

It took time for the debt to be fully cleared. But one day, Samir opened his old ledger and looked at it. The number that had once looked like a mountain to him had, by now, shrunk considerably.

"You know, if we had broken down that day, we would never have seen a day like today."Samir "Money lost can be earned again. A life lost cannot."Mita
The Lesson of the Story

Debt is never a small problem. A large debt can push a person into fear, shame, anxiety, and despair. But it helps to remember:

Debt is a problem — not a life sentence.

If Debt Is Weighing on You or Someone You Love

  • Talk to someone in the family you trust — don't carry it alone.
  • Write down every debt, in full: what's owed, to whom, and at what interest.
  • Contact your lender or creditor and ask whether a realistic repayment schedule or restructuring option is available.
  • Seek advice from a qualified lawyer, debt adviser, or financial professional where appropriate.
  • If you face harassment, threats, or unlawful pressure, seek help from an appropriate local authority or legal professional.
  • If you feel unable to cope or you are worried about your immediate safety, tell someone you trust and contact a qualified mental-health professional or your local emergency/crisis service.

But there's a debt — that doesn't mean life is over.
The road may be hard, but there is a road."

Keep life going, and look for the way through the problem. Because money can be earned again — a life cannot.
