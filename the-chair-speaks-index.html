The Philosophy of the Story

🪑 The most powerful thing about this story is this: the chair was never really a chair. It is a symbol of power, conscience, duty, and justice.

1. The Chair = Power

The chair a person sits in to make decisions is the very chair that one day demands an account of those decisions.

2. The Chair = Conscience

Even if no one else knows, a person always knows their own wrongdoing. The chair brings that hidden truth into the light.

3. The Chair = The People's Trust

A person who sits in a government chair does not sit upon their own property — they sit in the seat of a duty entrusted to them by the people.

4. Tapan Ray = The Crime of Silence

Not only the guilty — even the one who stayed silent while knowing a wrong was happening must, one day, stand before their own conscience.

5. Pradipta = A Symbol of Hope

The chair didn't give him power; it reminded him — duty comes before power.

"The chair does not judge people — a person, sitting in the chair, judges themselves."

"Who is next?"

It's best to leave the question open. Because that closes the mystery of the story — but not the philosophy of the chair.
