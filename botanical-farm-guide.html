How to Start — Step by Step
Choose 2–3 High-Demand Crops
Start with Tulsi + Lemongrass + Aloe Vera as your starter trio. These need minimal care, have strong local and export markets, and grow well in all of West Bengal including Bankura district.
Land Preparation & Sourcing Material
Prepare raised beds with organic compost at 5–8 t/acre. Source certified planting material from CSIR-NBRI Lucknow, CIMAP (Central Institute of Medicinal & Aromatic Plants), or your nearest KVK. Avoid uncertified roadside vendors for medicinal plants.
Cultivation & Gap Filling
Plant in monsoon (July–August) for best establishment. Tulsi: 30×30 cm spacing; Lemongrass: 60×60 cm; Aloe Vera: 45×45 cm. Weed control is critical in first 2 months. No heavy chemical fertilisers — organic preferred for market premium.
Harvest & Drying
Tulsi: harvest aerial parts every 60–75 days. Lemongrass: cut 15 cm above ground every 3 months. Aloe: harvest outer leaves after 6–8 months. Dry shade-dried at below 45°C to retain active compounds. Moisture below 10% for export.
Essential Oil Extraction (Advanced)
Steam distillation unit for Lemongrass/Citronella oil: small unit ₹50,000–1,50,000. 100 kg fresh Lemongrass yields 0.6–0.8 kg essential oil worth ₹800–2,500/kg. Get oil tested at CIMAP or private lab before selling.
Sell & Register
Sell dried herbs to local ayurvedic medicine manufacturers, traders in Kolkata's Burrabazar herb market, or bulk buyers via eNAM. For export: register with APEDA + AYUSH Ministry for herbal export certification. AGMARK certification raises your market value significantly.

Cultivation Tips — Marigold & Tuberose
Marigold — Easiest Start
Sow seeds in nursery trays in June–July. Transplant 45×45 cm at 25–30 days. First harvest in 70–80 days. Harvest before full bloom for longevity. Yield: 8–12 tonnes/acre. Sell to garland makers, flower mandis, wholesalers. Cultivate African Marigold (Tagetes erecta) for highest yield.
Tuberose — Premium Income
Plant bulbs March–April at 30×15 cm spacing. Single petal varieties (Shringar, Calcutta Double) for local market; Double petal for garland export. First flowering in 90–100 days. Bulb sets multiply — once established, minimal replanting needed. Yield: 8–10 tonnes spikes/acre.
Gerbera — Poly-house Value
Grow in poly-house for export-grade quality. Plant tissue-cultured plugs at 30×30 cm. Drip irrigate with fertigation. First harvest in 70–80 days; productive for 3–4 years. Yield: 60–80 stems/plant/year. Cold-chain pack in corrugated boxes for hotel and florist supply at ₹4–8/stem.

Hydroponic Systems Compared
🟢 Beginner Systems
NFT (Nutrient Film Technique) — thin film of nutrient solution flows over roots; ideal for leafy greens
Kratky Method — passive, no pump needed; roots sit in nutrient water with air gap; cheapest to set up
Deep Water Culture (DWC) — roots submerged in aerated nutrient solution; fast growth for lettuce

Dutch Bucket System — for larger fruiting crops (tomato, capsicum, cucumber)
Setup cost: ₹15,000–3,00,000; suitable for commercial production

How to Start Snail Farming
Choose Your Species
For export/cosmetics: Helix Aspersa Muller (Garden snail) or Achatina fulica (Giant African Snail). A. fulica is easiest to rear in Indian climate — grows to 200–500g, fast breeder. Source from established breeders or collect ethically from local habitats where abundant.
Set Up Enclosure
Use wooden boxes, mesh cages or small greenhouse-type enclosures (called snaileries). Ground level or raised bed: 1 sq m can support 50–80 adult snails. Shade net essential — snails are nocturnal and avoid direct sunlight. Maintain 75–80% humidity.
Feeding & Care
Feed: leafy greens, pawpaw leaves, banana, cooked cornmeal, carrots, squash. Add calcium source (crushed eggshell, limestone powder) for shell development. Feed 5% of body weight daily. Clean enclosure weekly — hygiene is critical to prevent disease outbreaks.
Breeding & Harvest
Snails are hermaphrodites — all snails can breed. One pair produces 200–400 eggs per year. Eggs hatch in 2–3 weeks. Snails reach market size (100–250g) in 8–12 months. Harvest by hand. For cosmetic mucin: stimulate secretion by gentle agitation or mild irritant — no harm to snail.
Market & Export
For local sale: connect with exotic food restaurants in Kolkata/Metro cities. For export: find buyers on Alibaba.com (search "snail suppliers Europe"). Register with APEDA. Snail mucin for cosmetics requires laboratory purification — partner with biotech companies or CSIR labs for processing.

Setting Up a Vermicompost Unit
Choose Location & Bed Type
Shaded outdoor area or shed. Make beds in brick tanks, HDPE tubs, or on cemented floor (1.5 m × 1 m × 0.6 m). Raised beds prevent flooding. Shade net above — worms die above 35°C and in direct sunlight. Investment: ₹3,000–25,000 for basic setup.
Source Earthworms
Eisenia fetida (Red wiggler) is best for vermicomposting. Purchase 1–2 kg of worms per bed from KVK, ICAR centres, or private sellers (₹200–400/kg). These reproduce every 60–90 days — one-time investment. Never use field earthworms — they do not compost efficiently.
Prepare & Add Feed Material
Mix cow dung + agricultural waste + kitchen waste in 2:1:1 ratio. Pre-compost for 15–20 days (hot composting) before adding worms — raw material is too hot for worms. Maintain 60–70% moisture — squeeze test: a handful should hold shape but not drip. Add material in thin layers every 10–15 days.
Harvest & Pack
After 45–60 days, surface becomes dark, crumbly and earthy-smelling — ready to harvest. Stop feeding 15 days before harvest so worms migrate down. Screen through 6mm mesh. Pack in jute bags or HDPE bags (25 kg). Vermicompost: ₹4–12/kg. Worm castings liquid (leachate): dilute 1:10 — spray on crops as liquid fertiliser.
Scale & Sell
10 beds (15 sq m): produce 1–2 tonnes/month — income ₹5,000–20,000/month. Sell to farmers directly, Krishak Bazaar, organic stores, eNAM portal. SHG of 10 women can manage 30–50 beds with ₹50,000 investment. State Horticulture Dept and NABARD provide 50–75% subsidy on vermicompost units.

How to Start a Home Processing Unit
Legal Setup (First & Most Important)
Get FSSAI Basic Registration (₹100/year for home-based micro unit). This is mandatory for any food product sale. For turnover above ₹12 lakh/year: FSSAI State License (₹2,000–5,000). GST registration optional below ₹20 lakh turnover. Udyam MSME registration: free, gives access to government schemes and loans.
Equipment for Beginners (₹10,000–30,000)
Stainless steel vessels (20–50 litre), weighing scale, refractometer (for jam/jelly sugar measurement), sealing machine (₹2,000–8,000), glass/PET bottles/jars, labelling printer. Start with 1–2 products only — master quality before diversifying.
Recipe Standardisation & Quality
Use FSSAI-approved recipes and preservative levels. Key rule: Jam must have minimum 68% TSS (total soluble solids measured by refractometer). Pickle oil-to-vegetable ratio and salt % determine shelf life. Get first batch tested at NABL lab before commercial launch. Consistency is what creates repeat customers.
Packaging & Branding
Attractive packaging is 60% of your retail success. Use food-grade glass jars. Create a brand name, logo (free on Canva). Labels must include: product name, ingredients, net weight, manufacturing date, best before date, FSSAI license number, and your address. Minimum label requirements by law.
Sell Online & Offline
Local: grocery stores, farmer's markets, school canteens, gift shops. Online: Amazon Easy Store, Meesho, WhatsApp Business, Instagram Shop. Niche opportunity: "homemade", "no preservatives", "Bengali traditional" positioning commands 2–3× premium. Gift hamper sets for Durga Puja, Diwali — seasonal business with high margins.
