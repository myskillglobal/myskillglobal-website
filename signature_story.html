II

The Weight of a Government Signature

"From the certificate of birth to the certificate of death, every important chapter of a person's life calls for a government document and a government signature."

A child's recognition at birth, a person's civic identity, education, employment, land and property, marriage, divorce, business, law, the courts — there is no counting how many of life's important decisions rest on a single government signature.

III

Whose Property Is This Signature?

This signature belongs to no one personally. It is the trust of the people.

When a government employee signs a document, he is not merely writing his name — he is representing his position, his duty, and the trust the state has placed in him.

IV

The Chain of Corruption

So when that signature is sold for money, it is not one document that becomes corrupt. Slowly, the whole system turns corrupt with it.

If land is wrongly approved today, someone's right will be taken away tomorrow. If a false document is signed today, an innocent person may be branded a criminal tomorrow. If a legal paper is changed for money today, the ordinary person's faith in justice itself will collapse tomorrow.

And a society where people have no faith in their government can never stand on healthy ground.

V

The Pen in Anik's Hand

So a government employee's pen may hold only a little ink, but his responsibility is never small. It must be remembered—

Selling a signature does not corrupt one employee alone; it corrupts the trust of the people.

And once that trust is broken, no power, no money, no law can easily bring it back.

Bikash Babu placed the pen in Anik's hand and said—

"Let this pen remind you — when power comes to you, you must not take its price. You must take its responsibility."

Anik lowered his head and pressed the pen to his chest. There were tears in Dipanwita's eyes too.
