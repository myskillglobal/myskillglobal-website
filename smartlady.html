Our story

Why we built Smart Lady

A woman plays many roles in a single day: managing the household budget, her children's schooling, the family's health, and her own safety. Yet clear, reliable, free guidance on all of these in one place is hard to find.

Smart Lady aims to fill that gap. There is no jargon here, only step-by-step explanations, small habits you can start today, and clear instructions for what to do in an emergency. The idea is simple: a woman who understands her safety, her money and her rights is truly independent.

The initiative is part of MySkillGame, a free educational platform whose goal is to bring life-ready learning, beyond the textbook, to everyone.

Safety

The first five things to do in an emergency

Staying calm in danger is the hardest part. A short plan made in advance is often the strongest protection you have.

  1. Move to a safe place. Head for a crowd, a lit shop, a police booth or the home of someone you know.
  2. Call 112. If you cannot speak, keep the call connected and put the phone in your pocket so your location can be traced.
  3. Tell two trusted people. Send a short message with where you are and what is happening.
  4. Preserve evidence. Do not delete messages, photos or vehicle numbers.
  5. Report when you are safe. Remember: it is never your fault.

What to prepare in advance

  • Save two trusted people in your phone under an "Emergency" label
  • Keep emergency info and SOS enabled on your lock screen
  • Before going somewhere unfamiliar, share the address and time with someone
  • When returning alone at night, share the ride's number and photo with someone
  • Traffickers often lure with good jobs, marriage or work abroad: tell family and verify before accepting any unknown offer

The Smart Lady app is being designed to make these steps as simple as one button. Right now you can switch on your phone's own Emergency SOS (usually under "Safety / Emergency" in Settings).

Money skills

Household budgeting: the simple 50-30-20 rule

Keeping accounts is not stinginess, it is control over your own money. The easiest way to start is to split monthly income into three parts.

50%Needs
30%Wants
20%Savings

Needs are rent, food, electricity, transport, loan instalments and schooling. Wants are eating out, hobbies and entertainment. In a low-income household 20% may not be possible: start with 5%. The start is what matters.

How big should an emergency fund be?

Money set aside for illness, job loss or sudden repairs is an emergency fund. A common guideline is 3 to 6 months of essential expenses.

  • First goal: one month of expenses
  • Keep it somewhere you can withdraw easily
  • Do not touch it for festivals or shopping
  • If you use it, refill it

Three questions before taking a loan

  • What is the interest rate and the total amount I will repay?
  • What share of my income is the monthly instalment?
  • Is the lender registered, and is a self-help group a better option?

The market list: a small habit, big relief

Forgetting one item at the market means another trip and extra cost. Divide your list: fresh market (vegetables, fruit), grocery (rice, lentils, oil, spices), fish, meat and eggs, and stationery (soap, notebooks, daily supplies). Send the list to whoever is shopping so nothing is missed. The Smart Lady app will include a market list you can build and share.

Self-reliance

Practical ways to earn from home

Think first of work you can start with little capital. The list below is only a suggestion: choose by your skills, local demand and the time you have.

Food-based work

Pickles, lentil dumplings, traditional sweets, tiffin service or cakes to order. Gauge local demand and start in small batches. Learn the licensing and hygiene rules for food businesses.

Crafts and tailoring

Tailoring, embroidery, kantha stitch, handicrafts. You can reach buyers with photos on social media, local fairs and self-help groups.

Teaching and digital work

Tutoring, data entry, translation, writing, design. A phone and internet are enough to begin. Always agree payment terms clearly before starting.

A small plan for starting a business

  1. Who will you sell to? Neighbours, schools, offices or online buyers: decide who you are serving.
  2. Work out costs and price. Include materials, fuel, packaging and the value of your time.
  3. Test in a small way. Get feedback from your first ten customers.
  4. Keep a separate ledger. Do not mix business income with household money.
  5. Look for support. Ask about government schemes, self-help groups and training centres.

Scheme rules, eligibility and application methods change from time to time. Before applying, verify the latest details with the concerned office or its official website.

Health and rights

Give your body and your rights the priority they deserve

Regular women's health habits

  • A general check-up at least once a year, including haemoglobin
  • See a doctor for big changes in your cycle or unusual pain
  • Ask your doctor about age-appropriate breast and cervical screening
  • Regular check-ups and vaccinations during pregnancy
  • Do not ignore constant tiredness or anxiety: talk about it and ask for help

This information is for general awareness and is not a substitute for medical advice. If you have symptoms, visit a nearby health centre or a qualified doctor.

Rights worth knowing

  • The right to open and run a bank account in your own name
  • The right to equal pay for equal work
  • Protection from sexual harassment at work, with an internal complaints committee
  • The right to seek help under the law against domestic violence
  • Access to free legal aid through the District Legal Services Authority
  • The right to have an FIR or complaint registered

Laws and their application can differ by state and situation. For a specific problem, consult a qualified lawyer or a legal aid centre.

Online safety

Eight habits for staying safe on your phone and online

  1. Never share OTPs, PINs or passwords. Banks and companies never ask for them over the phone.
  2. Do not click unknown links. Messages like "you won a lottery" or "update your KYC" are often scams.
  3. Use a screen lock and two-step verification (2FA).
  4. Be careful sharing photos and personal details. Never send intimate photos to someone you do not know.
  5. Keep social profiles private. Do not tag your location in real time.
  6. Install apps only from official stores and turn off permissions you do not need.
  7. If you are threatened or blackmailed, do not stay silent. Keep screenshots, block and report.
  8. If you suffer financial fraud, call 1930 immediately or report at cybercrime.gov.in. The first few hours matter most.

Privacy

Your information stays yours

The Smart Lady app is designed so that your income and expenses, savings, lists, emergency numbers and settings stay only on your own phone. No account is needed, and your personal accounts are not stored on our servers.

During an SOS, the idea is to use your phone's location to message the two people you chose. Nothing is tracked until you press the button.
