Why This Is Needed

The reason behind this market

At the market, it's easy to feel quietly helpless. From the outside you may look clean and presentable, but your pocket is nearly empty. All around, fish, fruit, meat, toys and daily essentials are laid out in plain sight — yet not everyone can afford to buy them.

The hardest moment comes when a young child asks for something. A child doesn't understand a household's income and expenses. All they have is a simple wish — "Baba, buy me that fish," or "Ma, I want that." And many parents have to quietly say, "Not today." That pain isn't only about money — it's about dignity too.

That is why an "Affordable Market" is needed — a place where families with limited income can buy good-quality fish, vegetables, lentils, rice, and other daily essentials at a lower price. This is not charity or a subsidy. It's an opportunity to shop at an affordable price while keeping one's dignity intact — made possible by sourcing directly from producers and cutting out unnecessary middlemen.

"Need is nothing to be ashamed of. Building a dignified, affordable market for everyone — that should be our goal."

Objective

The right to shop with dignity

The core goal of this initiative is simple: let people with limited income buy good-quality daily essentials at a low price, with dignity intact. This is not charity or a handout — it is a fair, direct system of selling.

How It Will Run

Not a subsidy — a fair supply chain

Sourcing

Directly from producers

Goods will be sourced directly from farmers, fishermen, and genuine producers, avoiding unnecessary hand-changes along the way.

Selling

Low margin, full transparency

Without any government subsidy, goods will be sold at a fair, low profit margin so prices stay within everyone's reach.

Standard

Savings without compromising quality

Even at a lower price, there will be no compromise on the quality of goods — that is the market's core promise.

Membership

Priority reaching the people it was built for

This market is built for ordinary villagers, daily-wage workers, farm laborers, rickshaw pullers, and lower-middle-income families — so no digital system is made mandatory. Simple methods are easier for people to adopt. The system is designed so that elderly people, those without formal schooling, or anyone less familiar with technology can use the market without any difficulty.

Membership

📖 Registry

A simple paper registration ledger

No app, no digital form — members will simply be registered by name in a plain paper ledger.

🪪 Card

A laminated member card, with photo

Every member will receive a simple, laminated card showing their photo and the number of people in their household.

🛒 Limit

A fixed weekly or monthly amount

Each week or month, members can buy a fixed quantity of goods based on the size of their household.

Selecting Members

🏛️ Referral

Recommended by panchayat, ward committee, or cooperative

Members will be selected based on recommendations from the local panchayat, ward committee, or cooperative society.

🔍 Verification

Verified locally

Membership will be granted based on local knowledge and verification — no complicated paperwork required.

📅 Review

Member list reviewed once a year

The membership list will be reviewed once a year and updated as needed.

Shopping

🪪 Show

Show the card, take the goods

Members simply show their card at the market to purchase their allotted amount of goods.

✍️ Record

Date and quantity noted in the ledger

Whatever is purchased is recorded in the ledger with the date — a simple, transparent record.

🚫 No app

No mobile app or QR code needed

Instead of modern technology, priority is given to a simple system everyone can understand.

"All of this market's operations will run on a simple, transparent system. Members will carry a basic identity card. Purchases will be recorded in a registration ledger. Rather than modern technology, priority will always go to a simple system that everyone can understand."
