From White Sari to Red Saree

“Don’t I Feel Hungry?” — A Little Girl’s Question That Stirred a Society

Introduction

In nineteenth-century Bengal, the winds of social change had begun to stir. On one side glowed the new light of education; on the other, many old customs still bound people's lives with an iron grip. The lives of widowed women, in particular, were hemmed in by countless social restrictions. This story is a work of fiction set against that historical backdrop. Jyotsna — a small girl. Widowed at a very young age. She was not yet old enough to understand what being a widow even meant; she only knew that she could not understand why, when everyone else ate, she alone had to go without. One Ekadashi afternoon, a simple question slipped from her lips — "Don't I feel hungry too?" That one question from a child changed the thinking of her elder brother, shook the beliefs of an entire family, and slowly carried her toward a future in which no one but she herself would decide the course of her own life. So this story is not only Jyotsna's story. It is a story of humanity standing against hunger, of conscience standing against custom, and of a girl reclaiming her own life.
