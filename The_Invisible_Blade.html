A Story About Kite Strings and the Cost of Winning

The Invisible Blade

A festival sky full of triumphant kites, a spool of glass-coated string, and a quiet road the next morning — a story about how something almost invisible can change everything.

About this story

“The Invisible Blade” is a short story set during a kite festival in the town of Chandipur. A boy named Rohan flies kite after kite with a spool of glass-coated string and wins. The next morning, something almost invisible on a quiet road changes the festival for everyone. The story looks at how a small choice made for fun, or to win, can hurt someone far away.

The story is divided into 9 short sections and takes only a few minutes to read. It is suitable for older children, students and families, and can start a talk about kite-flying safety and caring for people outside our own game. Press Listen to hear it read aloud (this uses your browser’s built-in voice, so the voice you hear depends on your device).

Content note: the story includes a fatal road accident caused by sharp kite string, with a brief mention of bleeding and a family’s grief. It is written for general readers, without graphic description. Parents and teachers may wish to read it first with younger children.

An Autumn Morning

The Sky Alive With Color

The sky above Chandipur was alive with color. Hundreds of kites danced in the cool autumn wind. Children shouted from rooftops.

“Cut it!” “Pull harder!” “My kite is the highest!”

Among them was thirteen-year-old Rohan, who dreamed of becoming the champion of the neighborhood kite festival.

This is special glass-coated manja, the shopkeeper said. It cuts every other kite.

That morning, Rohan had visited a crowded market where a shopkeeper proudly held up a shining spool of kite string. Rohan's eyes sparkled.

“I'll take it.”

By Sunset

Thirty Kites and a Hero's Feeling

The festival was everything he had hoped for. One by one, rival kites drifted helplessly through the sky. Each victory brought louder cheers.

By sunset, Rohan had cut more than thirty kites. He felt like a hero.

As darkness fell, he packed his kite and hurried home.

The broken strings remained hanging from trees… wrapped around electric poles… and stretched invisibly across nearby roads.

The Next Morning

A Promise of Chocolates

Nobody noticed. Nobody cared.

The next morning, schoolteacher Mr. Arindam Bose left home on his motorcycle. His daughter waved from the gate.

See you this afternoon, Baba!

He smiled beneath his helmet. “I'll bring your favorite chocolates.”

It was a promise he would never get the chance to keep.

On a Quiet Road

Almost Invisible

As he rode along a quiet road, something almost invisible floated across his path. Before he could react, the razor-sharp manja wrapped around his neck.

The motorcycle swerved violently. He crashed onto the roadside.

People rushed toward him. Someone called an ambulance. Another tried to stop the bleeding.

But the injury was too severe. By the time he reached the hospital, the doctors could do nothing.

That Same Day

A Photograph Beside a Candle

The news spread across the town within hours. The school closed for the day. Students stood silently outside the gates.

Rohan stared at the photograph placed beside a candle. His hands trembled.

The police later confirmed that a discarded glass-coated kite string had caused the fatal injury.

That night, Rohan couldn't sleep. Every time he closed his eyes, he heard children cheering as kites fell from the sky.

Those cheers now sounded painfully different.

A Week Later

The Public Safety Meeting

A week later, the town organized a public safety meeting. Doctors. Police officers. Teachers. Environmental volunteers. Everyone gathered together.

An injured cyclist spoke first.

A glass-coated string almost blinded me.

A bird rescue volunteer added that every year countless birds suffer terrible injuries because of these strings.

Then Mr. Bose's young daughter walked onto the stage. She held her father's broken spectacles in her hands.

My father promised to bring me chocolates. She paused. He never came home.

In That Silence

I Only Wanted to Win

The hall fell completely silent.

Rohan slowly stood up. Tears filled his eyes.

I bought that string. I never wanted anyone to get hurt. I only wanted to win.

He looked toward the audience.

I understand now… a game is never worth a human life.

From That Day

Two Reels, One Lesson

No one shouted. No one blamed him. Because everyone could see that the greatest punishment had already begun inside his own heart.

From that day onward, Rohan changed. He and his friends collected discarded kite strings after every festival. They organized awareness campaigns in schools and distributed safe cotton kite strings free of charge.

Years later, Rohan became an environmental engineer. Whenever he spoke to children, he carried two kite reels — one with ordinary cotton string, the other holding a short piece of banned glass-coated manja sealed inside a transparent box.

Can you tell the difference? he would quietly ask.

The children always answered, “They look almost the same.”

Rohan nodded.

Exactly. Many dangerous things in life look harmless. But wisdom is learning the difference before someone gets hurt.

Moral

The Sharpest Blade Is Almost Invisible

Every autumn, the people of Chandipur still filled the sky with colorful kites. Children still laughed. Families still celebrated. But one tradition had changed forever.

When the festival ended, everyone stayed behind. Together, they collected every broken string from rooftops, trees, and roads — because they had learned a lesson that no one wished to learn twice.

Sometimes, the sharpest blade is almost invisible.

The joy of a game should never endanger another person's life or harm wildlife.

Small acts of carelessness can have lifelong consequences, while small acts of responsibility can protect an entire community.

True victory is not defeating others — it is ensuring everyone returns home safely.

— what Rohan tells every child he meets, kite reel in hand

Think and talk about it

  • Why did Rohan feel like a hero at sunset, and how did that feeling change later?
  • Who can be hurt by a danger that is hard to see, besides the person flying the kite?
  • What did Rohan and his friends do after the festival to make things safer?
  • What simple rules would you want your family or class to follow on a kite-flying day?

This is a fictional story written to start a conversation. It is general safety awareness, not professional or legal advice. As the story shows, sharp or coated kite string can cause serious injury to people and animals. Choose plain cotton string, keep away from roads and power lines, and follow the rules and guidance of your local authorities.
