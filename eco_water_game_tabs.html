About this game

The EcoSmart Learning Game is a free, self-paced lesson and quiz about protecting water and soil. It has four short lessons, each with three slides, followed by a 10-question quiz. You can read the slides, listen to them read aloud, and then test what you remember.

It is written for students, teachers and families, especially in villages and small towns where drains, rainwater and household waste are part of everyday life. No sign-in is needed.

Water conservation: what the lessons teach

Water, soil and air are connected. Rain that cannot soak into the ground runs away, plastic that blocks drains floods streets, and smoke from burning waste settles on land and water. The four lessons follow this chain.

1. Soak pit: stop drain overflow, refill the ground

The problem: in concrete areas, rain and waste water have nowhere to go. Drains overflow, and standing water becomes a breeding place for mosquitoes, which spread diseases such as malaria and dengue.

The solution: a soak pit (safety chamber) built below a drain lets extra water seep slowly into the soil. It is filled in layers: coarse gravel and small stones catch debris, and sand filters fine particles before the water reaches the ground. The drain stays drier, mosquito breeding falls, and the underground water supply is topped up.

2. Rainwater harvesting: catch rain where it falls

The problem: where roads, roofs and yards are paved, rain runs into drains and on to rivers instead of refilling groundwater.

The solutions: rooftop rainwater can be led through pipes into a storage tank and used for gardening, washing and flushing toilets, which lowers the demand on piped or pumped water. Absorption pits and rainwater ponds let water enter the soil slowly. They are low-cost options for villages and small towns, reduce flooding, and help keep wells and ponds supplied in the dry season.

3. Plastic and soil: why litter hurts water

Plastic waste breaks into tiny pieces called microplastics. These can block the small pores in soil, so less water soaks in and soil life such as earthworms is harmed. Plastic that reaches drains blocks them and causes flooding, and in ponds and rivers fish and birds can mistake it for food.

What helps: use less single-use plastic, separate waste at home, compost kitchen waste, use cloth or jute bags, join clean-up drives, and plant trees near water bodies.

4. Burning plastic: never do it

Burning plastic releases toxic smoke, including chemicals such as dioxins and furans. Breathing it harms the lungs and over time can damage health in serious ways. Children are the most vulnerable. The smoke and ash settle on soil and water, and the next rain washes them into ponds and rivers.

What helps: refuse single-use plastic, use cloth or jute bags, compost food waste, and report open burning to the local authorities.

How to play: the rules

  1. Read the lessons first. The four tabs are Soak Pit, Rainwater, Soil & Plastic and Burning. Each has three slides. Use Prev and Next or tap the dots to move between slides, and tap Listen to hear a slide read aloud.
  2. Open the Quiz Game tab. You get 10 questions, one at a time. Each shows a picture from the lessons and four possible answers.
  3. Tap one answer. Your choice is final once you tap it. The right answer turns green. If you were wrong, your choice turns red. A short explanation always appears.
  4. Score a star for each correct answer. Every correct answer gives 1 point (⭐). There are no minus marks and no timer, so take your time.
  5. Tap Next Question. The bar at the top shows how far you have come.
  6. See your result after question 10. Tap Play Again to restart from question 1.

Your score is not saved. It stays only while the page is open, and the questions come in the same order each time.

Using what you learn in real life

At home

  • Keep drains and gutters clear. Do not throw plastic, oil or food waste into them.
  • Empty or cover anything that holds standing water (buckets, coolers, plant saucers, old tyres) at least once a week, so mosquitoes cannot breed.
  • Fix dripping taps and leaking pipes, and turn the tap off while brushing teeth or scrubbing dishes.
  • Use leftover water from washing rice or vegetables to water plants.
  • Carry a cloth or jute bag. Keep wet and dry waste apart and compost kitchen scraps.
  • Never burn plastic or mixed rubbish, and politely ask neighbours not to.

At school

  • Ask the school management and local authority about collecting rooftop rainwater or building a recharge pit.
  • Plant trees and grass around the playground and near any pond, so soil can take in water.
  • Organise a supervised clean-up of drains and pond banks, using gloves, with teachers present.
  • Use this quiz as a class activity and make posters on one lesson each.

In your village, ward or neighbourhood

  • Report blocked drains, standing water or open burning to the panchayat, municipality or local health office.
  • Support restoring ponds and building absorption pits where the ground allows it.
  • Share simple messages: reduce plastic, separate waste, never burn it.

Stay safe: soak pits, tanks and absorption pits should be planned with a trained local engineer or the local authority. The right design depends on soil, groundwater and where your drinking water comes from. Keep open pits and tanks covered or fenced so that children cannot fall in. Do not drink harvested rainwater unless it has been properly filtered and treated.

Frequently asked questions

Is the game free?

Yes. All lessons and the quiz are free, with no sign-up.

Who is it for?

Students, teachers, parents and anyone interested in water, soil and waste. The text is in English and every slide can be read aloud.

Why does a water game talk about plastic and burning?

Because they are linked. Plastic blocks drains and soil, so less rain soaks in, and smoke from burning plastic settles on land and water.

Are the figures in the lessons exact?

Numbers such as "up to 40%" or "up to 50%" are rough estimates to show scale. Real effects differ with place, soil, rainfall and design. For numbers that apply to your area, ask your local water or municipal office.

Is my score saved?

No. It is kept only while the page is open. The page uses Google Analytics to count overall visits and does not ask for your name.

Why does the Listen button sound different on my phone?

It uses the speech voice built into your browser, so the sound depends on your device. Tap the button again to stop it.

Can I build a soak pit or rainwater tank myself?

Please get advice from a local engineer, the panchayat or the municipality first. A badly placed pit can pollute a drinking-water source, and an uncovered pit or tank is a danger to children.
