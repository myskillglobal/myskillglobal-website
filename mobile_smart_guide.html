Mobile Smart Guide

About the Mobile Smart Guide

For millions of people in India the smartphone is the first and often only computer they own. It holds bank apps, photos, identity documents and family conversations, yet most people never learn how to buy a phone wisely, how to check whether a used phone is stolen, or how to protect their data. Scams that begin with an unknown app, a fake link or a public Wi-Fi network cost people their savings every day.

This page is an educational initiative of MySkillGame. It combines short explanations with a 9-chapter quiz (48 questions) and awareness pages on phone addiction, reels and social media, and the lifetime cost of owning a phone. Every quiz answer comes with a short explanation, so you learn the reason, not just the answer.

Smart phone habits, explained

1. Know your IMEI

The IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity) is a 15-digit number that identifies your handset. Dial *#06# to see it and compare it with the number on the box and the back of the phone. Keep a photo of it. If the phone is stolen, the IMEI is what lets it be blocked. In India you can check whether a phone is blacklisted or registered on the CEIR portal (ceir.gov.in).

2. Buying a used phone safely

  • The IMEI on the box, the phone body and *#06# should all match. A mismatch is a reason to walk away.
  • Ask for the original bill and meet in a public place.
  • Check battery health in settings (on iPhone) or with a battery-health app, and test the camera, speakers, charging port and touch screen.
  • A price far below the market rate usually means a stolen, damaged or fake phone.
  • Factory reset the phone before you start using it so no earlier owner's account or app remains.

3. Lock and protect your phone

Use a strong PIN or password, with fingerprint or face unlock only as a convenience on top of it. Turn on two-factor authentication for email and payment apps, install system updates, and back up photos and contacts. Never share an OTP or UPI PIN with anyone, including someone claiming to be from a bank or a delivery company. If you are a victim of an online financial fraud in India, the national cyber-crime helpline is 1930 and complaints can also be filed at cybercrime.gov.in; acting within the first hour improves the chance of recovery.

4. Before you sell or give away a phone

Back up your data, sign out of your Google or Apple account, remove the SIM and memory card, and then factory reset. Signing out first matters because some phones stay linked to the old account otherwise.

5. Radiation and health: what we know

Mobile phones emit low-level radio-frequency energy. Phones sold legally must stay within an exposure limit measured as the Specific Absorption Rate (SAR); India's limit is 1.6 W/kg. Major health bodies, including the World Health Organization, state that research to date has not established that phone use within these limits causes harm, while studies continue, especially on long-term heavy use. If you want to be careful, the simple steps cost nothing: use earphones or speaker for long calls, avoid keeping the phone against your body while sleeping, and prefer calls where signal is strong. This guide gives awareness, not medical advice.

6. If your phone is lost or stolen

  1. Call your operator to block the SIM, and use Find My Device (Android) or Find My (iPhone) to locate, lock or erase it.
  2. Report it to the police and keep a copy of the complaint.
  3. Block the handset using its IMEI through the CEIR portal.
  4. Change passwords and tell your bank so that UPI and banking apps on the phone can be disabled.

If you find a phone, do not try to unlock or use it; hand it to the police or the owner.

7. Unknown apps and links

Install apps only from the official store, read what permissions they ask for, and be suspicious of an app that wants access to your messages, contacts or screen without a clear reason. Do not install apps from links sent on WhatsApp or SMS, and never let a stranger make you install a screen-sharing or remote-control app. Instant-loan apps from unknown sources are a common source of harassment and fraud.

8. Public Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi at stations, cafes and markets can be monitored or faked. Avoid banking and password entry on it, prefer your mobile data for payments, make sure sites open with https, turn off automatic connection to open networks, and use a trusted VPN if you must work on public Wi-Fi.

9. Reels, short videos and screen time

Short-video apps are designed to keep you scrolling. Warning signs include losing track of time, restlessness without the phone, late-night use and neglecting studies, sleep or family. Practical steps: set a daily app timer, switch off non-essential notifications, keep the phone away an hour before bed, move social media apps off the home screen, and replace scroll time with a walk, a book or real conversation.

10. The lifetime cost of a phone

A phone costs more than its price tag: recharge, servicing, repairs and replacements add up over decades. The calculator in the app lets you enter your own numbers and see the total, which helps you choose a phone and a replacement cycle that suit your budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is this guide free?

Yes. There is no registration or payment, and the quiz needs no personal details.

Who is it for?

Students, parents, teachers and anyone who uses a smartphone, especially first-time smartphone owners. The language is kept simple.

How do I check my phone's IMEI?

Dial *#06# on the phone dialer. It is also printed on the box and, on many phones, under Settings > About phone.

Is mobile phone radiation dangerous?

Current scientific reviews have not established that phones used within legal limits cause harm, but research continues. Simple habits such as using earphones for long calls reduce exposure further.

What should I do first if my phone is stolen?

Block the SIM through your operator, lock or erase the phone with Find My Device or Find My, report to the police, and tell your bank.

Does the quiz store my answers?

No. Your score exists only while the page is open. The page uses Google Analytics to count visits, and does not ask for your name or contact details.
