An Awareness Story

One Mobile,
Three Lives

A story about family, pride, and why a life is worth more than any device.

Read aloud using your browser's built-in voice.

At the edge of a city there lived a small family — the father, Arun; the mother, Madhabi; and their eighteen-year-old son, Rahul. Arun held a modest job. There wasn't much comfort in the household, but the parents never fell short in trying to give their son a good life.

Rahul dreamed of owning a high-end smartphone. Seeing new phones in his friends' hands, he began to feel that without one of his own, he was falling behind everyone else.

One day, he bought an expensive phone on installments.

The first few months, the payments were managed somehow. But then money troubles set in, and Rahul asked his father for help.

"Son, right now it isn't possible for me to give this much money. There are so many household expenses."Arun, with a sigh "Can't you do even this much for me?"Rahul, in anger "If you truly need it, I will try. But we can't stop paying for the household's necessities just for a phone."Arun, trying to explain

The argument grew. Rahul felt that no one wanted to understand him. In a moment of anger, hurt, and despair, he left the house.

A little while later, word came — Rahul had gone to a dangerous hillside spot, in a state of intense emotional turmoil.

Arun and Madhabi rushed there. Local people came forward too, and everyone tried to calm him down.

"Rahul, come home. We'll sort out the phone problem later."a neighbour "Your life is the most important thing."another voice in the crowd

But in that moment, Rahul seemed trapped inside his own anger and despair. Arun tried to reach his son — as a father, he had only one thought: bring my son back safely.

The situation turned dangerous within moments. While trying to bring Rahul back to safety, Arun met with an accident. Madhabi, rushing into that same danger, found herself in grave peril too.

In the end, a family lost all three lives.

And the phone that had started such a great disturbance was left lying there, silent. No one ever used it again.

The Real Lesson

This story is not about blaming a phone.

Technology is a necessary part of our lives, and owning a good phone is no crime. The trouble begins when something becomes so important to us that we're willing to belittle our own life, our family's love, or our future for its sake.

Something else matters just as much: sudden anger, extreme despair, or talk of self-harm should never be brushed off as "drama." If a child says "I don't want to live anymore" or "I will end myself," the response should never be to scold or to leave them alone. Stay close, stay calm, and reach out quickly — to trusted family, and to the professional support that's needed.

A Few Words for Parents

  • It isn't always possible to meet every want of a child — but it's always possible to listen to what they say.
  • Not being able to afford something expensive is never a reason to humiliate your child.
  • If a child shows unusual anger, despair, or hints of self-harm, take it seriously — every time.
  • Rules around technology are healthy for a family, but they should come from love and understanding, not control.
"Are you trying to scare me by doing this?" "Tell me what you're feeling. I'm here with you."

For Children

Don't measure your own worth by what's in a friend's hand, or by what someone else is showing off online.

A phone can be replaced.
A thing can be bought again.
Money can be earned again.
But a life, once lost, cannot be brought back.

So don't carry the hardest moments of life alone.

Talk.  ·  Tell someone.  ·  Ask for help.  ·  Take your time.

Often, the problem that feels like the biggest problem in the world in that moment turns out, a few days later, to be just one small chapter of a much longer life.

Before you put an expensive phone in your child's hand, give them something more valuable first — the certainty that whatever life brings, they can always come back to their parents.

Life is bigger than any phone.
Family is worth more than any installment.
And a single human life is priceless.

If This Story Feels Close to Home

This is a sensitive topic. If you or someone you know is experiencing severe emotional distress or thoughts of self-harm, please don't face it alone.

Stay with the person, listen calmly, and reach out to a trusted family member, friend, teacher, counselor, doctor, or mental health professional. If there is immediate danger, contact your local emergency services or a crisis support service in your country. You are not alone. Help is available. Your life matters.
