This Life, Once Gone, Never Returns

Chapter 4: What I Gained, What I Lost

One never quite notices when time carries a person from one chapter of life into the next.

Ayan was working now.

He had his own income. Responsibilities toward his family. A salary arriving at the start of each month, and accounts to settle by its end.

The boy who once worried about his future with an exam sheet in hand now carried far bigger worries.

Household expenses. His parents' needs. Countless family responsibilities. Office work.

Taken together, the days seemed to have grown smaller.

Office from morning to evening. Home after that.

Sometimes sitting exhausted in front of the television, sometimes losing time on the phone for no reason at all.

One night, while tidying the cupboard, Ayan pulled out an old file.

Inside were his school report cards. Some college papers. Old certificates. And a photograph.

In the photo, he and Rahul stood together on the school field.

They had both been so young then.

Ayan smiled, looking at the picture.

Then, suddenly, the smile faded.

On the back of the photo, in Rahul's handwriting — “One day, we'll both become someone great.”

Ayan sat there quietly for a long while.

Rahul really had become someone great.

He had built his own company. Learned new things. Sharpened his skills, year after year.

And Ayan?

He wasn't doing badly. He had a job. A family.

But suddenly it occurred to him — had he actually used the full measure of his own ability?

The question left him uneasy.

Sleep did not come that night.

He lay in bed, staring up at the ceiling.

The small decisions of his life kept coming back to him.

The course he had meant to take, but never finished.

The book he had bought meaning to read, still sitting untouched in the cupboard.

The exam he was supposed to sit for, but skipped at the last minute for lack of preparation.

The opportunity that had come his way, which he had turned down for lack of faith in himself.

One memory after another kept surfacing.

None of them, on their own, was a very big mistake.

But that night, for the first time, Ayan understood just how much difference small, accumulated neglect could make.

The next day, he ran into Rahul at the office.

The two of them hadn't met in a long while.

Sitting over tea, chatting, Rahul asked, — “Do you still think about learning something new?”

Ayan smiled faintly and said, — “I do think about it.”

— “Then why don't you start?”

Ayan fell silent.

Rahul said, — “Don't make the excuse of not having time. Everyone's time is limited. The question is, what are we choosing to prioritize.”

The words lodged themselves in Ayan's mind.

He realized that, all this while, he had been blaming a lack of time.

But perhaps the truth lay elsewhere.

He had had the time.

He had simply never given a part of it the importance it deserved.

Back home that day, Ayan cleared off his own desk.

He took out his old books.

He opened a notebook and wrote on the first page — “Not an account of what I've lost. But how I use what remains.”

And below that, he wrote — “At least one hour a day, for myself.”

His life did not change completely from that day.

The next day there was still office. Still household work. Still exhaustion.

But by the end of the day, he kept that one hour for himself.

Sometimes he read a book. Sometimes he learned something new. Sometimes he finished an old, unfinished task of his own.

After a while, he understood — changing yourself doesn't always require some grand opportunity.

Even small stretches of time are enough — if you choose to give them their due.

But Ayan didn't know that the hardest reckoning of his life was still ahead.

Because his fortieth birthday was approaching.

And on that very birthday, a question would shake him in a way he could no longer avoid.

“Another year has gone by — what have I actually gained?”

The hands of the clock moved on.

And Ayan moved on too, toward that question waiting for him in his life.
