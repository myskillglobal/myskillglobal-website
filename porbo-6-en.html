Chapter 6: Time, Like a River's Current

After the night of his birthday, something strange had begun to shift in Ayan.

From the outside, nothing had changed at all.

Morning comes.

Office happens.

Household work goes on.

He meets people.

Night falls at the end of the day.

But Ayan no longer measured time only by the hands of a clock, the way he once had.

He had begun to feel time instead.

One day, on his way to office, he stopped at a street corner where he saw a small crowd gathered outside a shop.

Someone said,

— “Only two minutes left!”

Nearby, a football match was playing on a large screen.

One team was ahead by a single goal.

The last moments of the match.

The crowd's faces were taut with excitement.

One minute.

Then a few more seconds.

The opposing team was attacking desperately.

The very team that, until now, had wanted time to move forward, now wanted that time to run out as fast as possible.

Someone was shouting—

— “Referee, blow the whistle!”

Ayan stood and watched the scene.

It struck him how strange the value of time truly was.

The same single minute.

But depending on the situation, the worth of that very minute changes entirely.

Sometimes one minute feels impossibly long.

Sometimes an entire hour vanishes in the blink of an eye.

A few days later, another incident occurred.

Ayan was crossing the road on his way back from office.

Suddenly, a motorbike came speeding from the right, passing right beside him.

Ayan stepped back.

The bike sped off, a few feet away.

It all happened in a single instant.

He stood at the roadside for a while.

His heart pounding inside his chest.

Then, slowly, it occurred to him—

Just one second.

What if he had reached the middle of the road one second earlier?

What if the bike had arrived one second later?

The slightest shift, either way, and that evening could have turned out very differently.

Ayan looked up at the sky.

That day, time no longer meant hours, minutes, or seconds to him.

Time had become—

a thin line between life and death.

That evening, back home, Ayan sat on the veranda.

His daughter was studying.

Suddenly she said,

— “Baba, I have to submit my project tomorrow.”

Ayan asked,

— “Is it done?”

His daughter shook her head and said,

— “No. I was thinking I'd do it tonight.”

Ayan stayed quiet for a while.

Then he said, smiling softly—

— “Night will come, of course. But will time stop and wait for you?”

His daughter looked at her father.

Ayan said nothing more.

He understood that the lesson of time cannot be taught through a lecture.

Sometimes a late morning teaches it.

Sometimes an exam result.

Sometimes the last few minutes of a match.

And sometimes, a single second survived in an accident.

That night, Ayan stood by the window, gazing outside.

In the distance, light fell across the river.

The current of the water moved on, unceasing.

For a moment, it occurred to him—

Isn't human life just the same?

A river never looks back to see which stone lay where.

It simply keeps flowing.

And so does time.

Childhood passes.

Adolescence passes.

Youth passes.

The working years pass.

One day, retirement arrives too.

Whether we want it or not—every chapter of life ends in its own time.

Some moments we spend in joy.

Some in sorrow.

Some in busyness.

And some we simply lose, for no reason at all.

But time never keeps a separate account for anyone.

It simply moves on.

Ayan opened his notebook.

Beneath the three resolutions he had written on the night of his birthday, he wrote one more line today—

“Time cannot be held onto. But it can be made meaningful.”

He set down his pen and sat staring at those words for a long while.

It struck him that all this time, he had feared losing time.

From now on, he would try instead to make use of it.

Because the current of a river cannot be stopped.

But a person can, if he chooses, steer his boat in the right direction.

And perhaps the biggest question in life is just that—

Not how fast the current flowed. But in which direction we carried our lives through it.

Ayan did not know how many more years lay waiting for him ahead.

But today was his, in his hands.

And he made up his mind—

He would no longer simply let today pass by.
